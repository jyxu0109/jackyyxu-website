AI Percussion Practice Assistant

Building a more adaptive and musician-centered way to practice.

Why I Started

Practice should create understanding, not only repetition.

Students often recognize that something went wrong, but do not always understand where it happened, why it happened, or how to improve it.

During years of percussion study and teaching, Jacky observed that musicians frequently repeat difficult passages without receiving meaningful feedback between lessons.

Many existing practice tools focus on scores, percentages, or simple right-and-wrong judgments. This project begins with a different question: can technology respond more like a thoughtful practice coach?

The goal is not to replace teachers. It is to help musicians practice more independently, identify specific problems, and arrive at their next lesson with greater awareness.

The Product

Feedback designed around musical decisions.

The assistant analyzes a recorded percussion performance and translates technical data into clear, musician-friendly guidance.

Instead of presenting only a numerical score, it identifies the musical location that needs work, explains the issue, and recommends a focused next step.

Repeated takes can be compared so the system recognizes improvement, recurring weak points, and material that may be ready to move on.

Current Capabilities

A growing engine for percussion practice analysis.

01

Rhythm Recognition

Compares performed attacks and intervals with the expected rhythm.

02

Timing Analysis

Identifies early, late, short, and long rhythmic events at specific musical locations.

03

Adaptive Coaching

Recommends targeted practice based on recurring weak points and improvement across repeated takes.

04

Session History

Tracks previous performances and compares changes in timing, consistency, and musical focus.

05

Recording Quality

Evaluates whether the recording is clear enough to support useful musical analysis.

06

Rudiment Development

Expanding recognition and feedback for flams, drags, rolls, accents, and other percussion language.

Design Principles

01

Musical Language

Feedback should refer to measures, beats, subdivisions, technique, and musical intention.

02

Specific Feedback

Musicians need to know exactly where an issue occurred, not only that a performance was imperfect.

03

Actionable Practice

Every analysis should lead to a clear next step that can be used immediately.

04

Progress Over Scores

The system should recognize improvement and reduce emphasis on material the musician has already mastered.
